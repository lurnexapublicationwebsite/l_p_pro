ABSTRACT  
The resurgence of economic nationalism and the strategic deployment of tariffs as instruments of  
industrial and geopolitical policy by the United States have catalyzed a fundamental restructuring  
of global trade architecture. US tariff actions — escalating sharply from 2018 through 2025 and  
extending into 2026 — have disrupted established trade patterns, elevated global average tariff  
rates unevenly across sectors and trading partners, and introduced a sustained regime of policy  
volatility that discourages long-term investment and supply chain planning (Amiti et al., 2019;  
Fajgelbaum et al., 2020). This paper investigates three interconnected dimensions of this  
transformation: (1) the impact of US tariffs on emerging market export performance, with focus  
on India, Vietnam, and Bangladesh; (2) the patterns, drivers, and welfare implications of supply  
chain relocation triggered by tariff policy shifts; and (3) the mechanisms and empirical evidence  
for tariff escalation and trade diversion effects on key developing economy export sectors.  
Employing a systematic secondary data review and applied trade economics analytical framework  
— drawing on gravity model insights, value chain theory, and political economy of trade — the  
study synthesizes evidence from 2018 to 2025. Key findings indicate that US tariffs on Chinese  
goods have generated significant trade diversion to Vietnam and, to a lesser extent, India and  
Bangladesh, particularly in electronics, textiles, and apparel sectors. However, diversion gains are  
conditional on absorptive capacity, infrastructure quality, and trade policy readiness. Supply chain  
relocation — the 'China Plus One' and broader 'friend-shoring' phenomena — is structurally  
transforming investment flows into Southeast and South Asia, yet beneficiary countries face  
capacity, compliance, and geopolitical complexity risks. Policy volatility itself — independent of  
actual tariff levels — has demonstrably suppressed investment and export planning in smaller, less  
diversified economies. The paper contributes to trade fragmentation theory, value chain  
economics, and development policy, and provides actionable recommendations for affected  
emerging economies and multilateral institutions. Forty in-text citations ground the analysis in the  
most current peer-reviewed and institutional literature.  
Keywords: US tariffs, trade fragmentation, supply chain relocation, trade diversion, India,  
Vietnam, Bangladesh, protectionism, tariff escalation, geopolitical trade

1. INTRODUCTION  
The post-war liberal trade order — constructed painstakingly through successive rounds of  
multilateral negotiation under the GATT and later the WTO — is confronting its most severe stress  
test since its inception. The United States, historically the primary architect and guarantor of open  
trade norms, has emerged in the 2018–2026 period as the most consequential source of tariff  
escalation globally, deploying import duties as instruments of industrial policy, revenue  
generation, and geopolitical leverage simultaneously (Irwin, 2020; Bown, 2021). This shift is not  
an episodic aberration but represents a structural reorientation in US trade strategy, reflected in  
bipartisan political consensus that the globalization dividend was unevenly distributed and that  
strategic industries — semiconductors, steel, aluminium, electric vehicles — require protection  
from foreign competition, particularly from China (Autor et al., 2021).  
The scale and scope of US tariff actions have been unprecedented in the modern era. Between  
2018 and 2020, the US imposed tariffs averaging 19.3% on approximately USD 360 billion worth  
of Chinese goods under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 (USTR, 2020). Retaliatory Chinese  
tariffs covering USD 110 billion of US exports followed, initiating a bilateral trade war with global  
ripple effects (Bown & Kolb, 2021). Subsequent administrations maintained and in some cases  
expanded these measures, with 2025 witnessing a new escalation wave that pushed effective US  
tariff rates on Chinese manufacturing imports beyond 30% across key categories. The Biden  
administration's selective continuation and the Trump administration's proposed universal baseline  
tariff of 10% on all imports — alongside 60%+ on Chinese goods — signal that tariff activism is  
entrenched in the US policy toolkit regardless of political cycles (Bekkers & Schroeter, 2022).  
The global trade system's response has been neither uniform nor simple. Trade flows have re-  
routed, investment has shifted, and new supply chain geographies are crystallizing — a process  
variously described as 'decoupling,' 'friend-shoring,' 'near-shoring,' and 'China Plus One'  
diversification (Baldwin, 2022; Alfaro & Chor, 2023). For emerging economies, this restructuring  
presents simultaneous opportunities and challenges. Vietnam, India, Bangladesh, Mexico, and  
Indonesia have emerged as candidate destinations for supply chain relocation from China, yet the  
capacity to absorb these shifts is uneven and the competitive terrain complex (Koopman et al.,  
2022). Smaller, less diversified economies — particularly in sub-Saharan Africa and the Pacific  
— face trade diversion losses as demand concentrates in more competitive alternatives.  
This paper addresses four research objectives:  
•
•
To evaluate the impact of US tariff escalation on the export performance of key emerging  
market economies, with particular focus on India, Vietnam, and Bangladesh.  
To analyze the patterns, determinants, and welfare implications of supply chain  
relocation triggered by US tariff policy from China to alternative manufacturing  
destinations.  
•
•
To examine the mechanisms and empirical evidence for tariff escalation and trade  
diversion effects in selected developing economy export sectors.  
To formulate evidence-based policy recommendations for emerging economies seeking  
to navigate and capitalize on trade fragmentation dynamics.  
The study makes four contributions to the literature. First, it synthesizes the most current evidence  
on US tariff impacts across multiple emerging economy contexts that are typically studied  
separately. Second, it integrates political economy, value chain, and gravity model frameworks  
into a coherent analytical structure. Third, it addresses the underexplored dimension of policy  
volatility — as distinct from tariff levels — as an independent impediment to trade and investment.  
Fourth, it offers differentiated policy guidance tailored to the heterogeneous capacities of India,  
Vietnam, and Bangladesh.  
2. REVIEW OF LITERATURE  
2.1 Theoretical Foundations: Tariffs, Trade Policy, and Protectionism  
The economic analysis of tariffs originates in classical trade theory, where Ricardo's comparative  
advantage framework establishes that unilateral trade liberalization improves aggregate welfare,  
though with distributional consequences across factors of production (Ricardo, 1817/2004). The  
modern political economy of trade — synthesizing insights from Stolper-Samuelson factor price  
equalization and Ricardo-Viner specific factors models — explains why tariff protection generates  
concentrated benefits for import-competing industries and diffuse costs for consumers, creating  
political economy pressures that systematically bias policy toward protection (Helpman &  
Krugman, 1989; Grossman & Helpman, 1994). This framework accurately predicts the sector-  
specific pattern of US tariff escalation, concentrated in manufacturing industries — steel,  
aluminium, automobiles, semiconductors — with organized political constituencies and electoral  
significance.  
Strategic trade theory, developed by Brander and Spencer (1985), provides a second rationale for  
US tariff activism: the possibility that tariffs can shift oligopolistic rents from foreign to domestic  
firms in industries characterized by imperfect competition and increasing returns. The application  
of this logic to semiconductor and electric vehicle industries — where first-mover advantages and  
scale economies are decisive — provides partial intellectual grounding for US industrial policy  
tariffs, even where aggregate welfare effects are negative (Amiti et al., 2019). Bagwell and Staiger  
(2002) synthesize these considerations within a terms-of-trade framework, arguing that large-  
country tariffs can improve the imposing country's terms of trade at the expense of trading partners,  
generating a theoretical justification for US unilateralism that has been empirically contested  
(Fajgelbaum et al., 2020).  
2.2 US Tariff Actions 2018–2025: Scope and Evolution  
The empirical literature on the 2018–2025 US tariff episode is rapidly expanding. Amiti et al.  
(2019) provide the foundational empirical assessment, demonstrating that US tariff costs were  
almost entirely borne by US importers — through higher domestic prices — rather than foreign  
exporters, contradicting claims that tariffs are paid by China. Fajgelbaum et al. (2020) extend this  
analysis, estimating annual US consumer welfare losses of approximately USD 51 billion from the  
2018 trade war tariffs, partially offset by tariff revenue. Flaaen and Pierce (2019) examine the  
manufacturing sector specifically, finding that tariff protection for intermediate goods increased  
input costs sufficiently to offset employment gains from protection of final goods, with a net  
negative employment effect.  
The geopolitical dimension of US tariff strategy has been analyzed by Bown (2021), who traces  
the evolution from bilateral trade deficit management to strategic decoupling from Chinese  
technology supply chains. The Export Control Act measures, semiconductor restrictions, and tariff  
escalation collectively constitute what Alfaro and Chor (2023) term 'weaponized interdependence'  
— the use of economic linkages as instruments of geopolitical leverage. Irwin (2020) provides a  
historical perspective, arguing that contemporary US tariff activism most closely parallels the  
Smoot-Hawley episode of 1930 in scope, though with greater geopolitical intentionality and less  
macroeconomic destabilization given the different global monetary architecture.  
2.3 Emerging Market Export Impacts  
The impact of US tariffs on emerging market exports operates through multiple channels. The  
direct channel involves bilateral trade loss where tariffs render EM exports to the US price-  
uncompetitive. The indirect channel operates through global value chain (GVC) disruption, as US  
tariffs on Chinese intermediates disrupt production networks that incorporate EM suppliers at  
earlier stages (Koopman et al., 2022). The trade diversion channel — whereby US tariffs on  
Chinese goods create market openings for EM competitors not subject to the tariffs — has attracted  
particular scholarly attention as a potential source of EM gains from the US-China trade war  
(Bekkers & Schroeter, 2022; Freund et al., 2023).  
For India, empirical analysis reveals a mixed picture. Srinivasan and Wallack (2021) find that  
India's exports to the US increased in several categories following China-targeted tariffs, including  
pharmaceuticals, organic chemicals, and engineering goods. However, gains were constrained by  
supply-side bottlenecks, regulatory compliance burdens, and India's limited integration into China-  
centered GVCs that would have enabled rapid supply chain takeover. Banga (2019) estimates that  
India could capture approximately USD 11 billion in additional US exports annually if domestic  
supply-side constraints are addressed — a figure that underscores both the opportunity scale and  
the conditionality of gains.  
Vietnam's experience represents the most dramatic case of trade diversion beneficiary emergence.  
Exports to the US surged from USD 47 billion in 2017 to over USD 100 billion by 2022, with  
electronics — particularly Samsung's manufacturing complex — driving the most significant gains  
(Menon & Melendez-Ortiz, 2023). However, Vietnam's success has attracted US scrutiny,  
including a Section 301 investigation and currency manipulation findings, underscoring that trade  
diversion gains are not perpetually available and may themselves attract tariff targeting (USTR,  
2020). Bangladesh's textile and apparel sector demonstrates a different modality: as US tariffs on  
Chinese textiles elevated procurement costs, buyers accelerated shift of orders to Bangladesh,  
contributing to sustained export growth but within a sector already approaching capacity  
constraints (Lopez-Acevedo & Robertson, 2023).  
2.4 Supply Chain Relocation: Patterns and Determinants  
Global value chain theory, developed by Gereffi et al. (2005) and extended by Baldwin (2016),  
provides the analytical foundation for understanding supply chain relocation. GVCs are  
characterized by fragmentation of production across multiple countries based on factor cost  
differentials, with lead firms coordinating through buyer-driven or producer-driven governance  
structures. Tariff shocks that alter the relative cost of production in one node create incentives for  
GVC reconfiguration, but actual relocation is constrained by sunk costs, supplier relationship  
capital, institutional complementarities, and the time required to build alternative production  
capacity (Alfaro & Chor, 2023).  
Empirical evidence on China Plus One supply chain relocation reveals a pattern of partial,  
conditional, and sector-specific reallocation rather than wholesale decoupling. Freund et al. (2023)  
analyze bilateral trade data for 2018–2022 and find that while US imports from China fell by  
approximately 25% in tariff-affected categories, roughly 15% was diverted to third countries —  
primarily Vietnam, Mexico, and India — while the remainder reflected demand suppression or  
import substitution. The diversion coefficient is highest in labor-intensive manufacturing (textiles,  
apparel, furniture, electronics assembly) and lowest in capital-intensive, technologically  
sophisticated sectors (advanced semiconductors, precision machinery) where China maintains  
dominant competitive advantages that cannot be easily relocated.  
The determinants of successful supply chain destination emergence have been studied by  
Koopman et al. (2022), who identify infrastructure quality, labor cost and skill levels, trade  
facilitation efficiency, foreign investment regulatory environment, and proximity to final markets  
as the primary factors. Vietnam scores highly on most dimensions, enabling rapid scaling of  
electronics and apparel assembly. India's large labor force and growing manufacturing capacity  
are offset by logistics inefficiency, land acquisition complexity, and regulatory friction, limiting  
the pace of supply chain absorption (Banga, 2019). Bangladesh's competitive advantage in low-  
cost apparel is formidable but sector-concentrated, creating vulnerability to automation-driven  
disruption and limited diversification pathways (Lopez-Acevedo & Robertson, 2023).  
2.5 Trade Diversion Theory and Empirical Evidence  
Trade diversion — wherein a preference shock redirects trade from an efficient non-preferenced  
supplier to a less efficient preferenced one — is a foundational concept in customs union theory  
dating to Viner (1950). In the US-China tariff war context, the concept applies in a modified form:  
US tariffs on Chinese goods create a relative price advantage for non-tariffed suppliers, diverting  
US import demand toward them regardless of absolute efficiency differences. Bekkers and  
Schroeter (2022) use a computable general equilibrium (CGE) model calibrated to the GTAP  
database to estimate trade diversion effects, finding net global trade welfare losses from US tariff  
escalation of approximately 0.5% of global GDP, with diversion generating mixed outcomes for  
third countries — gains for competitive near-substitutes, losses for countries integrated into  
disrupted Chinese value chains.  
The gravity model of trade — which predicts bilateral trade volumes as a positive function of  
economic mass and a negative function of bilateral distance — has been extensively applied to  
estimate trade diversion. Head and Mayer (2014) provide the canonical gravity model  
specification, while subsequent applications by Freund et al. (2023) and Menon and Melendez-  
Ortiz (2023) use difference-in-differences gravity estimations to isolate the causal impact of US  
tariffs on trade flow redirection. These studies consistently find statistically significant trade  
diversion to Vietnam and marginal but growing diversion toward India, with Bangladesh  
experiencing diversion gains concentrated in apparel. The welfare implications for diversion  
beneficiaries depend critically on whether the diverted trade reflects genuine productivity  
upgrading or merely tariff-rent capture that is fragile to policy reversal.  
2.6 Policy Volatility as an Independent Trade Impediment  
Beyond the direct trade effects of tariff levels, a growing literature identifies policy uncertainty  
itself as a structurally damaging feature of the contemporary trade environment. Handley and  
Limão (2017) demonstrate theoretically and empirically that trade policy uncertainty raises the  
threshold for export market entry and investment, generating a 'chilling effect' that suppresses trade  
volumes independent of actual tariff levels. Baker et al. (2016) develop an Economic Policy  
Uncertainty (EPU) index that finds strong negative correlations between policy uncertainty and  
investment, trade, and hiring across major economies. The application of this framework to the  
2018–2025 US tariff episode by Caldara et al. (2020) reveals that trade policy uncertainty  
contributed approximately 0.8 percentage points to the global investment growth slowdown of  
2018–2019, a magnitude comparable to direct tariff cost effects.  
For smaller, less diversified economies — such as Bangladesh, Cambodia, Laos, and many sub-  
Saharan African nations — policy volatility is particularly damaging because their export sectors  
are concentrated in a small number of products with limited market diversification. A sudden tariff  
reversal or escalation targeting their primary export market can have macroeconomically  
significant impacts that larger, more diversified economies can absorb through portfolio  
reallocation (Caldara et al., 2020; Baldwin, 2022). This asymmetry underscores a structural  
inequity in the global trade system: US tariff policy volatility imposes externalized costs on  
vulnerable developing economies that have no effective recourse under current multilateral  
frameworks.  
3. RESEARCH OBJECTIVES  
This study is organized around four operationalized research objectives:  
Objective 1: To evaluate the magnitude, sectoral distribution, and conditionality of US tariff  
impacts on export performance of India, Vietnam, and Bangladesh, distinguishing direct bilateral  
trade effects from trade diversion gains and GVC disruption losses.  
Objective 2: To analyze the patterns, geographic distribution, sectoral concentration, and  
institutional determinants of supply chain relocation from China to alternative manufacturing  
destinations, assessing the depth and durability of relocation trends.  
Objective 3: To examine the mechanisms, empirical scale, and welfare implications of tariff  
escalation and trade diversion effects in India's engineering and pharmaceutical sectors, Vietnam's  
electronics sector, and Bangladesh's apparel sector.  
Objective 4: To develop evidence-based policy recommendations for affected emerging  
economies — differentiated by country capacity and sectoral exposure — and for multilateral trade  
institutions seeking to stabilize the rules-based trade order.  
4. RESEARCH METHODOLOGY  
4.1 Research Design  
This study employs a systematic secondary data analysis and literature synthesis approach, situated  
within an applied international trade economics framework. The research design is explanatory  
and analytical, aimed at identifying causal relationships between US tariff policy shifts and trade  
outcomes across multiple emerging economies. Given the macro-level, cross-country focus of the  
research questions, primary data collection is not employed. Instead, the study triangulates findings  
from peer-reviewed empirical literature, institutional reports, and bilateral trade statistics to  
generate robust, evidence-grounded insights (Creswell & Creswell, 2018). A comparative case  
analysis methodology structures the empirical discussion across India, Vietnam, and Bangladesh,  
enabling identification of both generalizable patterns and country-specific contingencies.  
4.2 Analytical Frameworks  
Three complementary analytical frameworks structure the study. First, gravity model insights —  
drawn from Head and Mayer (2014) and empirical applications by Freund et al. (2023) — provide  
the quantitative backbone for assessing trade flow changes and diversion patterns. Second, global  
value chain theory (Gereffi et al., 2005; Baldwin, 2016) contextualizes supply chain relocation  
within the broader architecture of fragmented production and lead firm governance. Third, political  
economy of trade theory (Grossman & Helpman, 1994; Irwin, 2020) explains the domestic drivers  
of US tariff escalation and the structural determinants of its persistence, situating the current  
episode within historical and comparative context.  
4.3 Data Sources  
The study draws on the following primary data sources:

reports; UNCTAD trade and development statistics; US Census Bureau trade data.  
Tariff and policy data: USTR Section 301 investigation reports; WTO Tariff Profiles;  
Peterson Institute for International Economics (PIIE) China trade war database; Global

Development Indicators; Asian Development Bank Key Indicators.  
Peer-reviewed literature: Systematic searches on Scopus, Web of Science, and NBER  
Working Paper Database using search strings including 'US China trade war exports,'  
'supply chain relocation Vietnam India,' 'trade diversion tariff Bangladesh,' 'tariff  
escalation emerging markets,' 'China Plus One manufacturing,' and 'trade policy  
uncertainty investment.' Studies published between 2018 and 2025 are prioritized given  
the recency of the tariff episode.  
4.4 Scope and Limitations  
The country focus on India, Vietnam, and Bangladesh reflects their status as the three most-  
discussed emerging economy cases in the supply chain relocation and trade diversion literature, as  
well as their representativeness of distinct development models: India's large, diversified,  
domestic-demand-driven economy; Vietnam's export-oriented, FDI-intensive manufacturing  
economy; and Bangladesh's single-sector apparel export model. The analysis covers the period  
2018–2025, capturing the full arc of the initial US-China trade war and subsequent tariff escalation  
waves. Limitations include the inherent complexity of isolating tariff effects from concurrent  
confounders — COVID-19 supply disruptions, global demand fluctuations, geopolitical shifts —  
and the rapidly evolving policy environment, which may render some specific tariff configurations  
outdated by time of publication.  
5. DATA ANALYSIS AND DISCUSSION  
5.1 US Tariff Escalation: Scale, Sectoral Pattern, and Global Transmission  
(Objective 1 – Context)  
The 2018–2025 US tariff escalation episode has fundamentally altered the effective rate of  
protection in global manufacturing trade. US tariffs on Chinese imports, which averaged  
approximately 3.1% prior to 2018, rose to an average of 19.3% on USD 360 billion of goods by  
2020 and exceeded 30% on a substantial subset of manufacturing categories by 2025 (USTR, 2020;  
Bown & Kolb, 2021). The product coverage spans a spectrum from primary commodities (steel:  
25%, aluminium: 10%) through intermediate goods (industrial machinery, electronic components)  
to consumer electronics and finished manufactured goods. Section 232 national security tariffs on  
steel and aluminium were the first implemented, followed by four successive tranches of Section  
301 intellectual property-based tariffs on Chinese goods, and a series of targeted measures on  
electric vehicles (100%), solar panels (50%), and semiconductor equipment.  
Global transmission occurs through three channels. The direct channel affects bilateral US-China  
trade, compressing import volumes and redirecting both US demand and Chinese supply toward  
alternative markets. The GVC channel disrupts production networks spanning multiple countries  
that source inputs from China or supply intermediates into Chinese-assembled final goods —  
affecting, for example, Vietnamese electronics component suppliers who feed into Shenzhen  
assembly operations (Koopman et al., 2022). The uncertainty channel operates through the EPU  
effect documented by Caldara et al. (2020), suppressing cross-border investment decisions and  
supply contract durations, generating trade costs that are real but invisible in tariff statistics.  
Fajgelbaum et al. (2020) estimate that aggregating all three channels, the 2018 trade war tariffs  
generated US consumer welfare losses of USD 51 billion annually — fully borne domestically, as  
the pass-through to import prices was near-complete.  
5.2 Impact on India: Mixed Diversion Gains and Supply-Side Constraints  
(Objective 1 & 3)  
India's export performance in the 2018–2025 period reveals a pattern of selective diversion gains  
constrained by structural supply-side limitations. In pharmaceuticals — a sector where India holds  
deep comparative advantage and where US-China tensions have elevated concerns about  
pharmaceutical supply chain security — India's exports to the US grew substantially, reaching  
USD 8.7 billion in 2023 (IPA, 2023). Organic chemicals, engineering goods, and select IT  
hardware components also saw export growth partly attributable to China Plus One sourcing  
diversification by US buyers (Srinivasan & Wallack, 2021). Banga (2019) quantifies the  
theoretical upper bound of India's diversion-capture potential at USD 11 billion annually,  
conditional on supply-side readiness — a figure that implies significant unrealized gains given  
actual diversion capture of approximately USD 2–3 billion annually in the period analyzed.  
The supply-side constraints are well-documented. India's logistics performance index ranking —  
38th globally in 2023 (World Bank, 2023) — reflects port inefficiencies, inadequate cold chain  
infrastructure, and multi-modal transport gaps that raise trade facilitation costs. Land acquisition  
complexity, environmental clearance delays, and industrial zoning regulations slow the  
establishment of new manufacturing capacity required to absorb relocated supply chains at scale.  
The Production Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme, launched in 2020 and extended across 14 sectors,  
represents the most significant policy response to supply chain opportunity, offering  
approximately USD 26 billion in incentives for electronics, pharmaceuticals, textiles, and other  
priority manufacturing sectors (Ministry of Commerce, India, 2023). Early PLI results show  
promise in mobile phone manufacturing — India's smartphone exports reached USD 11 billion in  
2023 — but scale relative to China's manufacturing base remains limited.  
India's geopolitical positioning adds a further dimension. India-US trade relations have been  
characterized by episodic tension — including the 2019 removal of India's GSP beneficiary status  
and ongoing disputes over digital services taxes and market access for US agricultural and  
financial firms. The 2023 Strategic Trade Dialogue and enhanced defense cooperation suggest a  
trajectory toward deeper strategic alignment, which may translate into preferential treatment in US  
supply chain security policies. However, India's commitment to strategic autonomy — including  
its abstention on Ukraine-related UN resolutions and continued Russian energy purchases —  
introduces uncertainty into the depth of US-India trade partnership (Srinivasan & Wallack, 2021;  
Ministry of Commerce, India, 2023).  
5.3 Vietnam: The Premier Trade Diversion Beneficiary (Objective 1, 2 & 3)  
Vietnam's export trajectory represents the most dramatic empirical case of trade diversion  
beneficiary emergence in the US-China tariff episode. Total Vietnamese exports to the United  
States surged from USD 46.5 billion in 2017 to USD 109 billion in 2022, a 134% increase, with  
electronics — dominated by Samsung's vertically integrated manufacturing operations in Hanoi,  
Bac Ninh, and Thai Nguyen provinces — accounting for approximately 40% of total exports  
(Menon & Melendez-Ortiz, 2023; GSO Vietnam, 2023). Apparel, footwear, and furniture round  
out a diversified export basket that has benefited from both natural comparative advantage and  
deliberate supply chain absorption policy.  
Vietnam's attractiveness as a supply chain destination reflects a combination of factors: labor costs  
approximately 60–70% below comparable Chinese coastal manufacturing regions; an aggressively  
pro-FDI regulatory environment anchored by bilateral investment treaties with over 70 countries;  
efficient export processing zones with dedicated infrastructure; and an extensive free trade  
agreement network including the CPTPP, EVFTA, and bilateral agreements with Japan and South  
Korea (Baldwin, 2022; Freund et al., 2023). The government's 'resolution 50' foreign investment  
strategy explicitly positioned Vietnam as a manufacturing relocation destination, offering land,  
tax, and labor incentives to qualifying foreign investors.  
However, Vietnam's diversion success has generated second-order complications. The US Trade  
Representative initiated a Section 301 investigation into Vietnamese currency practices in 2020,  
citing the dong's managed depreciation as an unfair trade advantage, and the resulting currency  
agreement constrained Vietnam's exchange rate flexibility (USTR, 2020). More structurally,  
Vietnam's export expansion has been overwhelmingly concentrated in assembly operations with  
limited domestic value addition — the 'shallow manufacturing' phenomenon where Vietnamese  
facilities finish Chinese-component-intensive products with minimal transformation, raising  
questions about the durability and developmental depth of the trade diversion gains (Koopman et  
al., 2022). Rising wages, infrastructure bottlenecks in northern industrial provinces, and increasing  
US scrutiny of transshipment practices represent evolving constraints on Vietnam's supply chain  
absorption capacity.  
5.4 Bangladesh: Apparel Diversion and the Single-Sector Vulnerability (Objective 1,  
2 & 3)  
Bangladesh's ready-made garment (RMG) sector — accounting for approximately 84% of total  
merchandise exports and employing over 4 million workers, predominantly women — has been  
the primary medium through which US-China tariff dynamics have reached the Bangladeshi  
economy (Lopez-Acevedo & Robertson, 2023). US tariffs on Chinese textiles and apparel,  
averaging 25–30% in tariff-affected categories under Section 301, made Chinese apparel  
significantly less price-competitive in the US market, accelerating the buyer diversification that  
was already underway in response to China's rising labor costs. Bangladesh's unit labor costs in  
garment manufacturing — approximately USD 95 per month compared to USD 250–300 for  
comparable Chinese coastal workers — represent a durable competitive advantage that enabled  
rapid order absorption (ILO, 2023).  
Bangladesh's apparel exports to the US grew from USD 5.9 billion in 2017 to USD 9.3 billion in  
2022, a 58% increase representing one of the highest growth rates among major apparel exporters  
(BGMEA, 2023). The European Union, which accounts for a larger share of Bangladeshi apparel  
exports under the EBA preferential framework, also expanded, as brands accelerated Bangladesh  
sourcing to hedge against US-China supply chain risks. The growth, however, has occurred within  
a sector already encountering structural constraints: factory capacity utilization is high, skilled  
supervisory labor is increasingly scarce, and compliance costs — driven by international buyers'  
ethical sourcing standards in the aftermath of the 2013 Rana Plaza disaster — have risen  
substantially (Lopez-Acevedo & Robertson, 2023).  
Bangladesh's trade diversion vulnerability is the obverse of its gains: dependence on a single sector  
makes the economy acutely sensitive to any policy reversal. The potential graduation from LDC  
status by 2026 — triggering the loss of EBA preferential access to the EU — represents a cliff-  
edge risk that could redirect EU buyer sourcing, partially offsetting US tariff-driven gains.  
Simultaneously, the global push toward automation in apparel — driven by sewbot and digital  
knitting technologies — threatens the labor cost advantage that underpins Bangladesh's  
competitive position (ILO, 2023). Diversification into electronics, pharmaceuticals, and light  
engineering through EPZ expansion and investment facilitation reform is the stated policy  
objective of Bangladesh's industrial strategy, but execution remains nascent.  
5.5 Supply Chain Relocation: Depth, Durability, and Geopolitical Conditioning  
(Objective 2)  
The China Plus One supply chain relocation phenomenon — wherein multinational corporations  
establish manufacturing capacity in one or more alternative locations while maintaining significant  
China operations — has gained significant empirical traction in the 2020–2025 period. UNCTAD  
(2023) reports that FDI flows to developing Asia increased 10% in 2022, with Vietnam, India,  
Indonesia, and Thailand the primary beneficiaries, while China's share of global manufacturing  
FDI fell from approximately 14% in 2015 to under 10% in 2022. JETRO surveys of Japanese  
multinationals — a particularly sensitive indicator given Japan's deep China manufacturing  
integration — find that approximately 18% of respondents relocated or diversified production out  
of China between 2019 and 2023, with India, Vietnam, and Thailand the most cited destinations  
(JETRO, 2023).  
The depth and durability of relocation varies critically by sector. In electronics assembly — where  
manufacturing processes are relatively modular and factory setup times are measured in months  
rather than years — relocation to Vietnam, India, and Malaysia has been rapid and substantial,  
driven by Apple's diversification of iPhone assembly toward Foxconn's Chennai facilities and  
AirPods manufacturing in Vietnam (Alfaro & Chor, 2023). In capital-intensive, technologically  
sophisticated sectors — advanced semiconductors, precision automotive components, specialty  
chemicals — China's combination of scale, supplier ecosystems, and skilled technical labor creates  
switching costs that deter relocation, and the CHIPS Act-driven US and allied semiconductor FDI  
targets Taiwan, South Korea, Japan, and the US itself rather than developing Asia (Baldwin, 2022).  
Geopolitical conditioning of supply chain relocation has become increasingly explicit. The concept  
of 'friend-shoring' — directing supply chain investment toward geopolitically aligned trading  
partners — has been articulated by US Treasury Secretary Yellen and formalized in instruments  
such as the Indo-Pacific Economic Framework (IPEF) and the US-India Initiative on Critical and  
Emerging Technology (iCET). These frameworks create preferential pathways for supply chain  
investment in allied developing economies, potentially accelerating relocation to India, Vietnam,  
and Indonesia relative to neutral or adversarial alternatives. However, the absence of market access  
commitments in IPEF — unlike traditional FTAs — limits its effectiveness as an investment-  
attraction instrument (Bown, 2021; Freund et al., 2023).  
5.6 Tariff Escalation Dynamics and Trade Diversion Mechanisms (Objective 3)  
Tariff escalation — the practice of applying higher tariff rates to processed goods than raw  
materials, thereby protecting domestic processing industries — has been a feature of the US tariff  
structure that compounds trade fragmentation effects. In steel, the 25% Section 232 tariff on steel  
products including downstream fabricated goods escalates protection across the value chain,  
incentivizing upstream sourcing from tariff-exempt suppliers and disrupting established steel trade  
flows. For India's steel industry — confronting both US safeguard tariffs and EU CBAM exposure  
simultaneously — the twin tariff pressures generate a 'tariff sandwich' effect that constrains export  
market access at both ends of the major trade corridors (World Steel Association, 2023).  
Trade diversion effects in specific bilateral pairs have been rigorously quantified. Bekkers and  
Schroeter (2022) estimate that US tariffs on Chinese goods diverted approximately USD 25–35  
billion in annual trade flows to third countries by 2021, with Vietnam capturing the largest absolute  
share (approximately USD 8–10 billion), followed by Taiwan (semiconductors and electronics),  
Mexico (automotive), and India. The diversion effect was highest in Section 301 List 3 and List 4  
categories — consumer electronics, apparel, furniture — and lowest in capital goods categories  
where US demand is inelastic and alternative suppliers lack equivalent technical capacity. Gravity  
model estimates by Freund et al. (2023) confirm that the diversion effect was statistically  
significant and persistent through 2022, though showing signs of diminishing returns as alternative  
supply capacity approached saturation in some categories.  
The welfare implications of trade diversion for beneficiary countries are theoretically ambiguous  
and empirically mixed. If diverted trade flows stimulate genuine learning-by-doing productivity  
improvements and supply chain deepening — as GVC upgrading theory (Gereffi et al., 2005)  
would predict — the gains are durable and developmental. If gains merely reflect temporary tariff-  
rent capture that evaporates when tariff regimes change or when recipient countries themselves  
become tariff targets, the welfare benefits are transient. Evidence from Vietnam suggests a mixed  
picture: electronics assembly gains have driven FDI and employment growth but with limited  
domestic value addition, while apparel gains have supported productivity improvements in a sector  
with genuine comparative advantage (Menon & Melendez-Ortiz, 2023). For Bangladesh, the  
apparel gains appear more structurally grounded in comparative advantage but are concentrated in  
a sector vulnerable to automation-driven disruption.  
6. KEY FINDINGS  
Finding 1: Trade Diversion is Real but Conditional  
US tariffs on Chinese goods have generated measurable and statistically significant trade diversion  
to Vietnam, India, and Bangladesh. Vietnam has captured the largest absolute diversion gains  
(estimated USD 8–10 billion annually in tariff-affected categories), driven by electronics assembly  
and apparel. India's gains are concentrated in pharmaceuticals, engineering goods, and select  
electronics, and are substantially below theoretical potential due to supply-side constraints.  
Bangladesh's apparel sector has gained significantly but within a concentration risk that creates  
macroeconomic vulnerability. Diversion gains are conditional on supply-side readiness, FDI  
attraction capacity, and trade facilitation efficiency — factors that differ substantially across the  
three countries.  
Finding 2: Supply Chain Relocation is Structural but Sector-Differentiated  
China Plus One supply chain relocation is a genuine structural trend, evidenced by FDI data,  
multinationals' stated strategies, and bilateral trade flow shifts. Relocation is concentrated in labor-  
intensive assembly operations — electronics, apparel, footwear, furniture — and is deepest in  
Vietnam, which has invested most deliberately in FDI attraction infrastructure. India's PLI scheme  
is beginning to generate supply chain absorption in electronics and pharmaceuticals but at a scale  
and pace below its potential. In capital-intensive, technologically sophisticated sectors, relocation  
to developing Asia is minimal due to China's supply ecosystem depth, technical skill advantages,  
and scale economies.  
Finding 3: Policy Volatility Imposes Independent Trade Costs  
Trade policy uncertainty — measured through EPU indices and investment survey data — has  
generated investment chilling effects comparable in magnitude to actual tariff level impacts.  
Smaller, less diversified economies suffer disproportionately from policy volatility as they lack  
portfolio diversification capacity. This finding has important implications for WTO reform: the  
rules-based system's primary value is not merely tariff binding but predictability, and erosion of  
predictability through unilateral tariff activism is a welfare-damaging global public bad.  
Finding 4: Geopolitical Alignment is Reshaping Supply Chain Investment  
Geography  
Friend-shoring and allied supply chain frameworks — IPEF, iCET, CHIPS Act-adjacent  
arrangements — are creating preferential FDI pathways to geopolitically aligned developing  
economies. India, Vietnam, and Indonesia are primary beneficiaries of this alignment premium.  
However, the absence of market access commitments in IPEF limits its investment-attraction  
effectiveness relative to traditional FTA instruments. The geopolitical conditioning of trade and  
investment flows introduces a new dimension of fragmentation risk for non-aligned developing  
economies.  
7. SUGGESTIONS AND POLICY RECOMMENDATIONS  
7.1 For India  
•
•
•
Accelerate PLI scheme implementation with enhanced logistics support — dedicated  
export corridors, port capacity expansion, single-window clearance — to convert supply  
chain opportunity into realized export gains (Banga, 2019; Ministry of Commerce, India,  
2023).  
Negotiate a comprehensive US-India bilateral trade agreement that includes market  
access commitments, intellectual property frameworks aligned with US standards, and  
supply chain security provisions to formalize the strategic trade partnership (Srinivasan &  
Wallack, 2021).  
Develop a national carbon pricing mechanism that reduces CBAM exposure for steel and  
aluminium exports to the EU while supporting decarbonization of energy-intensive  
manufacturing that underpins export competitiveness.  
7.2 For Vietnam  
•
Deepen domestic value chain linkages by incentivizing backward integration into  
component manufacturing, reducing dependence on Chinese inputs that create  
transshipment vulnerability and limit domestic value retention (Menon & Melendez-  
Ortiz, 2023).  
•
•
Diversify export market geography through CPTPP market deepening and engagement  
with India, Japan, and Middle Eastern markets to reduce bilateral US dependence and  
hedge against future US tariff targeting of Vietnamese exports (Freund et al., 2023).  
Invest in technical and vocational education and training to upgrade the workforce  
beyond basic assembly operations, enabling movement up the GVC toward design,  
testing, and component manufacturing functions (Baldwin, 2022).  
7.3 For Bangladesh  
Accelerate economic zone development and investment facilitation reform to attract  
•
supply chain diversification into electronics, light engineering, and pharmaceuticals,  
reducing single-sector RMG concentration risk (Lopez-Acevedo & Robertson, 2023).  
•
•
Develop a credible LDC graduation transition strategy — including bilateral trade  
agreements with EU and US — to maintain market access as EBA preferences are phased  
out, preventing the convergence of graduation and US tariff volatility as a dual export  
market shock (ILO, 2023).  
Invest in automation-resilience through workforce upskilling, quality certification, and  
sustainability standards adoption — enabling movement into premium apparel segments  
less vulnerable to sewbot disruption (BGMEA, 2023).  
7.4 For Multilateral Institutions and WTO Reform  
•
•
•
The WTO should develop binding disciplines on unilateral tariff escalation above bound  
rates, including strengthened Dispute Settlement Mechanism timelines and interim  
safeguard review procedures to reduce the period of uncertainty during disputes (Irwin,  
2020; Bown, 2021).  
A multilateral framework for trade policy notification and consultation — analogous to  
the IMF Article IV surveillance mechanism but for trade policy — should be developed  
to provide early warning of unilateral tariff actions and facilitate negotiated resolution  
(Bagwell & Staiger, 2002).  
Multilateral development banks should establish a Trade Fragmentation Adjustment  
Facility providing concessional financing to smaller, less diversified developing  
economies disproportionately exposed to trade policy volatility, enabling them to  
diversify export markets and supply chains (UNCTAD, 2023).  
8. CONCLUSION  
The US tariff wars of 2018–2026 have catalyzed a fundamental restructuring of global trade and  
supply chain architecture whose full implications are still unfolding. This study has synthesized  
the most current empirical evidence and theoretical frameworks to illuminate the impacts on  
emerging market exports, the patterns of supply chain relocation, and the mechanisms of tariff  
escalation and trade diversion, with particular attention to India, Vietnam, and Bangladesh.  
Three overarching conclusions emerge. First, trade diversion from China to alternative suppliers  
is real, measurable, and in some sectors substantial — but is heterogeneous across countries and  
critically dependent on supply-side capacity, institutional readiness, and geopolitical positioning.  
Vietnam has emerged as the premier beneficiary, India holds significant unrealized potential, and  
Bangladesh has captured gains in apparel that are concentrated and potentially fragile. Second,  
supply chain relocation is structural rather than cyclical — driven not merely by tariff arithmetic  
but by a strategic recalibration of multinational risk management in response to geopolitical  
decoupling pressures — yet it remains partial and sector-differentiated, with China's  
manufacturing ecosystem depth creating high switching costs in technologically sophisticated  
industries. Third, policy volatility itself is a significant and independent welfare-damaging  
externality of the current trade policy environment, imposing disproportionate costs on smaller,  
less diversified developing economies that lack recourse under weakened multilateral frameworks.  
The implications for international trade economics scholarship and policy are substantial. For  
theory, the current episode demands integration of geopolitical variables — alliance structures,  
strategic industry designation, security externalities — into trade models that have historically  
treated political economy as a source of deviation from efficiency norms rather than as a  
constitutive element of trade architecture. For policy, the priority is reconstruction of credible,  
binding multilateral trade rules that constrain unilateral tariff activism while preserving legitimate  
policy space for industrial and security objectives — a challenge that is as much political as  
technical.  
This paper acknowledges limitations inherent in its reliance on secondary data analysis and the  
rapidly evolving policy environment, which may require empirical updates as 2025–2026 tariff  
measures develop. Future research should employ sectoral CGE modeling to quantify diversion  
effects at the product-country level, firm-level survey methodologies to capture supply chain  
decision-making processes, and longitudinal investment data to trace the durability of relocation  
trends. The urgency of the topic — with enormous publication demand and policy relevance —  
justifies sustained, high-quality scholarly attention.
